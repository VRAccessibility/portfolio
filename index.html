Craig Anderton

Award-Winning HCI PhD Candidate A11y Researcher

Objective Statement

I am currently researching haptic technologies for sensory augmentation in immersive environments, with the aim of helping to make virtual reality more accessible to people with sensory impairments. I have contributed to high-impact publications in leading conferences and journals, including winning the Student Research Competition Graduate Gold Medal at ASSETS '22 as a Master's student, a first author publication in the Q1 IJHCI journal within my first year of PhD research, and a first author full paper at CHI '25, all of which underscores my commitment to advancing accessible and innovative digital experiences. With a background marked by academic excellence, distinguished awards, and a lived experience of disability, I am dedicated to leveraging my interdisciplinary expertise to drive transformative research and practical solutions in accessible immersive technologies.

Featured Research

Asleep at the Virtual Wheel: The Increasing Inaccessibility of Virtual Reality Applications (CHI '25)

This paper analyses 330 of the most-used commercial VR apps, revealing common accessibility failures and providing a framework for more inclusive design. doi - PDF - Video - Poster

From Teleportation to Climbing: A Review of Locomotion Techniques in the Most Used Commercial Virtual Reality Applications (IJHCI '24)

In this work, we reviewed locomotion methods in popular VR apps, categorising them to understand current trends and identify opportunities for more accessible techniques. doi - PDF - Poster

Investigating Sign Language Interpreter Rendering and Guiding Methods in Virtual Reality 360-Degree Content (ASSETS '22) - SRC Graduate Gold Medal 🥇

This award-winning research explored methods for presenting sign language interpreters within 360-degree videos to improve accessibility for d/Deaf users. doi - PDF - Video - Poster
